The weekly business review

HackerRank · 2025

An Amazon-style WBR for HackerRank’s Community platform, rebuilt so it was ready before anyone asked for it.

5 hrsof manual reporting saved every week

One review, the whole funnel, in order

I owned the weekly business review for the B2C Community platform. It was modelled on Amazon’s WBR: every week, walk the whole funnel in the same order.

Acquisition, activation, the aha moment, retention, churn and monetization. Same metrics, same definitions, every single week, so the conversation could be about what changed.

The order the review walked, every week

  1. AcquisitionWho signed up, and from where
  2. ActivationWho did the first meaningful thing
  3. Aha momentWho got the value we promised
  4. RetentionWho came back, read as 12-week cohorts
  5. ChurnWho stopped, and when
  6. MonetizationWho paid

Retention, read as 12-week cohorts

Retention got the most time in the room, and we read it as 12-week cohorts. Each week’s new users, followed week by week for 12 weeks.

Cohorts make it obvious where people drop off. You can line up the same week across every cohort and see if it’s getting better.

And you can follow a single cohort across the 12 weeks and see where that group lost interest.

From 5 hours by hand to already there

Before, putting the review together meant about 5 hours a week of pulling numbers by hand.

I built the pipeline from Redshift into staging tables and on into a self-serve Metabase dashboard. Product and GTM leadership could open it themselves, any day of the week.

Around the same time I moved the org’s reporting off Looker and Redash onto Metabase, so the review and everything else read from one place.

The pipeline behind the review

  2. Staging tablesShaped for the review, refreshed on schedule

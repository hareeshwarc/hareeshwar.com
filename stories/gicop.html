Compliance reports a regulator could trust

Mu Sigma · 2021

A 3-layer ETL for General Insurance Code of Practice reporting on vulnerable customers. It earned me a Spot Award.

100%consistent data across every report

What the reporting had to show

The insurer had to report on how it protects vulnerable customers under the General Insurance Code of Practice (GICOP).

The data for that lived all over the place: claims in one system, policies in another, and more besides. None of it was built to be reported together.

Three layers

The conform layer brought every source, claims data, policy data and the rest, into one platform.

The facts and dimensions layer turned it into a star schema. PII got masked here. Each entity had its dimensions, and the fact table held the full join.

The reporting layer turned the IDs in the fact tables back into information the business could read, ready for executive dashboards in Tableau.

Three layers, from raw systems to a report

  1. SourcesClaims data, policy data and the rest, each from its own system
  2. Conform layerEverything brought into one platform
  3. Facts and dimensionsA star schema. PII masked here. Dimensions per entity, the fact holds the join
  4. Reporting layerIDs turned back into information the business can read
  5. Executive Tableau dashboardsVulnerable-customer reporting the insurer could stand behind

Why it earned a Spot Award

The award came down to smooth execution and 100% consistency in the data. For regulatory reporting, anything less means someone has to explain a number to a regulator.

I drove a major section of the project to completion, and I automated the data processing and validation along the way so the checks ran every time, by themselves.

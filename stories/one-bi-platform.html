One place for every number

Hiver · 2026

Moving all of Hiver’s BI reporting onto Metabase, and paying for each question once a day instead of every time someone asks it.

>90%lower query cost

Every team had its own version

Reporting at Hiver lived in a few places: Looker, Superset, and dashboards that queried the data lake on AWS directly.

The decision was easy to say and slow to do. Metabase becomes the one source of truth for BI reporting, for the whole company.

And it happened. Every dashboard in Hiver moved. Superset and Looker logins were switched off after.

Where a number could come from

  1. LookerOffIts own model of the data
  2. SupersetOffA second set of dashboards
  3. Straight to AthenaMovedDashboards querying the data lake directly
  1. MetabaseOne source of truth for BI reporting, for the whole company

Running both until the numbers matched

Nothing got switched off on day one. Every dashboard ran in Metabase right next to its old version.

The old dashboards pulled from different sources, so they didn’t agree with each other, let alone with the new ones. Reconciling them was most of the work, and honestly most of it was data engineering.

An old dashboard only went away once both sides showed the same number.

Paying for a question once a day

The old dashboards hit the data lake through Amazon Athena, and Athena bills for the data each query scans: $5 per terabyte at AWS’s list price.

So every dashboard load, every filter change and every refresh was a fresh bill for the same data.

We moved the reporting tables into Postgres and refreshed them once a day. Athena now scans each table once a day, and after that people can query Postgres as much as they like without adding to the bill.

The math is simple. If a table is queried N times a day, its scan cost drops to 1/N. Anything queried 10 or more times a day saves at least 90%. Play with the slider.

Athena bills for data scanned, at $5 per TB on AWS's list price. Postgres runs on a fixed instance, so extra queries there don't add to the bill.

What it looks like now

Raw data sits in the S3 data lake. Glue and Athena read it once a day into Postgres, and Metabase reads from there.

Customer Success got their account health dashboard rebuilt on it, the one they use with clients. Leadership got the executive revenue dashboard: ARR, expansion and churn.

The tradeoff is freshness. Numbers are as fresh as the last refresh, once a day. For BI reporting, that’s the right call.

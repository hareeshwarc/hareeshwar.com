The claim that crossed a state line

Mu Sigma · 2021

Linking policy and claim data across Australian states, when the data was masked by design.

2new data sources for the fraud model

The gap

In Australia, insurance could be handled independently from state to state. Say someone buys a policy in one state.

Then they move to another state.

There, they raise a stolen vehicle claim and get reimbursed.

The state where the policy was issued didn’t connect to the state where the claim was raised. An easy door for fraud.

Connecting those data sources was the fix we came up with.

The gap a claim could slip through

States are placeholders. The pattern is real, the specifics are not shown.

Masked by design

The hard part: all of the data was masked. By design, policy data and claim data from two different states weren’t meant to connect.

We did solve it in the end, and could trace a person’s journey from the policy being issued to every claim they made after it.

What it fed

I ran the exploratory analysis that surfaced 2 new data sources for the insurer’s claims fraud detection model, and they improved its flag rate.

With the tech lead, I also built and maintained the Jenkins pipelines that ran the production fraud detection system.

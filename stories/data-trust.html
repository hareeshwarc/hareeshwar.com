The quarter where revenue didn’t move

Hiver · 2026

A customer health dashboard showed a flat line for a whole quarter. At Hiver’s scale, that just doesn’t happen.

0.04%daily gap to billing, after the fix

The line that didn’t move

I was going through the customer health dashboard, the one Customer Success uses on calls with clients. Over a whole quarter, revenue sat completely still.

No new business. No churn. No renewals. Every customer that quarter supposedly ended on the exact revenue it started with.

At the scale Hiver operates, that’s impossible. So the dashboard wasn’t telling me about the business. It was telling me something upstream was broken.

Customer health dashboard, revenue across one quarter

Drawn from memory to show the shape. No real values.

15 months, frozen

I went further back. The flat stretch wasn’t one quarter. Revenue history had been frozen for 15 months, for every customer.

It had already put wrong numbers into an executive report. And every dashboard reading that table inherited the freeze, which is how it went unnoticed. The numbers still looked like numbers.

I took it to data engineering with the specifics: which table, since when, and what it should have looked like. Together we found the pipeline bug feeding it and got it fixed.

Checking it against something I didn’t build

A fix isn’t done until it matches a source you don’t control. For revenue, that’s the billing system, so I checked the two against each other day by day.

On an ordinary scale, the gap that’s left doesn’t show up at all.

Zoom in twice and you find it: 0.04%. That’s close enough to put in front of leadership again.

Then the duplicates

While I was in there, the totals still felt a little high. Set the true figure at 100, and the dashboards were reporting 104.5.

Duplicate records were inflating revenue by 4.5%. Small enough to pass a glance, big enough to change a forecast.

They were removed at the source, so every dashboard downstream starts from the real number. ChartMogul had its own version of this too, blank customers and duplicate rows in the sync, and that fix went out as a PR.

What I took from it

Every number on a dashboard is a claim about the business. A flat line at a growing company is a bug until someone proves otherwise.

Now the first thing I ask of any dataset is what it should look like if the business is behaving normally, and then I check whether it does.

This is also the work I wrote about in Hiring a product analyst won’t fix broken data. It’s unglamorous, and everything else depends on it.
